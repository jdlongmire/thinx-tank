## Permitting bill one vote from passage

The Senate spent Wednesday clearing the path for the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act. Three votes landed in sequence: budget points of order waived 70 to 26, the Capito substitute amendment adopted 69 to 27, and cloture on the bill invoked 65 to 31. What remains is final passage, which needs a simple majority and is expected Thursday evening, with the House after that.

Why it matters: this is the permitting rewrite. Its timelines govern how fast energy and infrastructure projects move, and that includes the data centers and generation projects the defense AI buildout is waiting on. The votes are recorded in [the Senate Wrap Up for Wednesday, November 18, 2026](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/2026/11/18/wrap-up-for-wednesday-november-18-2026).

## Housing starts up on paper, permits down

October housing starts came in at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1,309,000, up 9.9 percent from the revised September figure of 1,191,000. The Bureau attaches a confidence interval of plus or minus 16.8 percentage points to that change, and the interval includes zero, so the increase is not statistically significant. Permits, the forward indicator, fell 7.4 percent to 1,295,000, and single family permits fell 8.7 percent to 772,000.

Why it matters: starts measure work begun, and permits measure work coming. A soft permit line points to a thinner construction pipeline into 2027, which is the housing side of the same capacity question the permitting bill addresses. The figures are from [the Census Bureau and HUD New Residential Construction release for October 2026, CB26-187](https://www.census.gov/economic-indicators/).

## Fed minutes: more hikes expected, AI financing flagged

The Federal Open Market Committee held the target range at 3.75 to 4 percent in October on a 10 to 2 vote, with the two dissenters preferring an increase. The minutes, released Wednesday, record that almost all participants judged further increases likely to be appropriate under their baseline outlooks, and a couple raised the possibility of a move larger than 25 basis points in December if inflation data disappoint. The staff discussion of financial stability named the AI buildout directly: debt growth, off balance sheet leasing, and circular financing among AI firms as amplifiers of risk.

Why it matters: the cost of capital for the AI buildout is set in this room, and the committee is now on record treating the buildout's financing structure, not only its output, as a stability variable. The full text is in [the minutes of the October 27-28, 2026 meeting](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcminutes20261028.pdf).

## Treasury designates a Hizballah gold and cash network

Under Operation Economic Outcast, the Office of Foreign Assets Control designated the Wa Ta'awanu Association and its founder Afif Abdel Rida Chouman, brothers Hassan and Ibrahim Khechen, and the companies Gold Global Company and Sankari Gold. Treasury says the network moved funds from Iran's IRGC-Qods Force to Hizballah in Lebanon and moved gold bullion and cash out of Africa. The designations, taken under Executive Order 13224, block United States property and bar United States persons from dealing with the named parties.

Why it matters: sanctions work lands on compliance desks. Any firm screening counterparties in gold, cash logistics, or Lebanon linked charitable channels has new names to match as of Wednesday. The designations are in [Treasury press release sb0692](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0692).

## GAO: four platforms produced feasible attack plans

The Government Accountability Office published the results of covert tests run in spring 2026 against Claude, Grok, ChatGPT, and Gemini. On every platform the safeguards fired first: requests denied, information removed, accounts suspended. Investigators then bypassed the safeguards using common techniques, and each platform produced information that GAO's experts judged feasible for planning an attack, in under 12 hours per platform: two improvised explosive device scenarios and two electrical grid scenarios. GAO withheld which platform handled which scenario and made no recommendations.

Why it matters: this is the first government run, published evidence that consumer platform safeguards fail against a determined tester, and it arrives while Congress is writing frontier AI legislation. Its limits are stated in the report itself: one test per platform, spring 2026 versions, not a ranking of today's systems. The report is [GAO-27-108814](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814).

## The implication

One vote in the Senate now sets the permitting timelines for the physical plant of defense AI. In the same news cycle, the Federal Reserve recorded the financing of that plant as a stability concern, and the GAO recorded the misuse risk of the models it will run. Build speed, financing risk, and misuse risk are now all on the official record at the same time. Planning that treats them as separate problems will misprice all three.
