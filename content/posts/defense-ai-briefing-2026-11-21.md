## Executive summary

- The Government Accountability Office published GAO-27-108458, a technology assessment titled "AI and Cybersecurity: Technologies Can Magnify Cyberattacks and Enhance Cyber Defense," on November 20. GAO found no widely accepted criteria for evaluating generative AI's offensive or defensive capabilities, and set out four policy goals with 16 policy options for policymakers. The options are a menu, not requirements.
- Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control designated four Iranian individuals on November 20 under Operation Economic Outcast (press release sb0697): two under Executive Order 13902 for operating in the petroleum sector, and two under Executive Order 13224, as amended, for supporting Sepehr Energy and Mahan Air. U.S. property is blocked.
- The Department of War's November 20 contract release named 58 companies to an Air Force multiple award IDIQ for advanced propulsion work, with a shared ceiling of $16.1 billion and work possible through November 2041. Only $58,000 was obligated at award, $1,000 per awardee.

## GAO: no shared yardstick for AI in cyber offense or defense

GAO's assessment, prepared by its Science, Technology Assessment, and Analytics team, found that AI technologies have given threat actors the ability to magnify the reach and impact of traditional cyberattacks: high quality phishing and deepfake content, more precise attacks, and, as an emerging trend, agentic AI carrying out multiple stages of an attack with minimal human input. GAO cited a June 2026 incident in which a threat actor used multiple AI agents to build a testing framework that refined malware evasion techniques against different security products. On the defense side, GAO found AI helps defenders analyze network traffic, identify vulnerabilities in source code, and process large volumes of security information, while introducing its own risks, including training data poisoning, model weight extraction, and prompt injection. GAO identified four policy goals: address gaps in cybersecurity guidance and practices, address AI related research gaps, govern AI tools throughout their cybersecurity lifecycle, and enhance the cybersecurity workforce. The 16 options include expanding AI testing environments, research into agentic AI identity management, validating AI tool outputs, and human authorization for consequential AI driven security actions. This is a verified GAO finding set; the policy options are proposals for Congress, agencies, standards bodies, and industry to consider, not rules.

AI industry implication: evaluation is the missing product. Vendors sell offensive and defensive capability claims that buyers currently cannot compare on common criteria, and GAO has now said so in a primary source. Defense implication: every AI security tool bought for a defense network inherits that gap. Procurement that does not demand test criteria, lifecycle governance, and a human authorization point for consequential actions is buying on assertion. The report is at [the Government Accountability Office](http://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108458).

## Treasury designates four Iranian oil network figures

OFAC's November 20 action targets what Treasury calls oil trustees, the insiders Tehran relies on to sell oil abroad and recover the proceeds. Mohammad Javad Bavand, a former IRGC intelligence officer, and Majid Taj were designated under Executive Order 13902 for operating in the petroleum sector of the Iranian economy, as part of a network tasked with obtaining cash from Iran's oil reserves and recovering funds held in third countries. Mohammadhossein Servati, who held executive posts at companies run by Sepehr Energy Jahan Nama Pars Company, the designated front company that sells oil for the Iranian Armed Forces General Staff, and Mojtaba Arabnejad, tied to Mahan Air and to aircraft parts and weapons procurement, were designated under Executive Order 13224, as amended. Treasury states that the regime is attempting to recoup billions of dollars from trustees who in many cases kept the revenue, and that no Iranian oil has been loaded onto vessels since Operation Economic Outcast began on August 24. The loading claim is Treasury's, stated here as the department stated it.

AI industry implication: limited and indirect. The action adds four names to the screening lists that export control and compliance systems, including AI driven screening tools, must match. Defense implication: this is continued financial pressure on the revenue line that funds the Iranian military and its proxies, applied to the collection network rather than to tankers, which fits a blockade posture where shipments have already stopped. The action is in [Treasury press release sb0697](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0697/).

## Air Force names 58 firms to a $16.1 billion propulsion vehicle

The November 20 Department of War contract release awarded 58 companies a multiple award indefinite delivery, indefinite quantity contract for advanced propulsion enterprise work, described in the notice as providing for the continuing enhancement of propulsion technology across all areas and lifecycle phases of propulsion development. The shared ceiling is $16.1 billion, the acquisition was competitive, Air Force Materiel Command at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base is the contracting activity, and work is expected to be complete by November 2041. Awardees include Boeing, General Electric, Honeywell, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, RTX, Rolls-Royce North American Technologies, Safran Defense and Space, Aerojet Rocketdyne, and Dynetics, alongside younger firms including Hermeus, Mach Industries, Beehive Industries, and Whisper Aero. The $16.1 billion is a ceiling, not obligated funding: $58,000 in fiscal 2027 research, development, test and evaluation funds was obligated at award.

AI industry implication: none direct, and that is the point worth recording. The pacing item for future air platforms is propulsion physics and industrial capacity, not software. Defense industrial base implication: a 15 year vehicle with 58 holders keeps both the established engine primes and the startup propulsion firms inside one competition pool. The funded task orders, not the ceiling, will show which firms the Air Force builds on. The award is reported from the Department of War release by [Breaking Defense, via The Vanguard USA summary of the release](https://thevanguardusa.com/8917/defense/air-force-58-companies-16-1-billion-advanced-propulsion-contract-2041/).

## The implication

GAO has put in writing that the United States has no widely accepted criteria for judging what AI does in a cyber fight, on offense or on defense. Until that yardstick exists, defense buyers are purchasing AI security tools they cannot compare, against attackers who face no such limit.
