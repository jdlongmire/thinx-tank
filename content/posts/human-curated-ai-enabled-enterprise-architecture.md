An enterprise architect spends a surprising amount of time finding out what the enterprise already knows. Strategy sits in one place, application inventories in another, and the reason for a standard may live in someone's memory. Before evaluating a change, we have to assemble enough of that picture to understand its consequences.

I put together this short video for a developing architect. Its central idea is that Enterprise Architecture maintains intentional coherence as an enterprise changes. GenAI can help with the information work around that responsibility. People still own the judgment.

{{< mentoring-video >}}

The graphic follows strategy through architecture, roadmaps, standards, and delivery. These relationships matter more than any individual diagram. A proposed system may solve a team's immediate problem while duplicating a capability, introducing conflicting data definitions, or creating a dependency nobody has agreed to support. Architecture makes those consequences visible before they become expensive commitments.

AI can help retrieve relevant decisions, compare proposals with standards, draft viewpoints, and identify relationships worth investigating. But a plausible relationship is still a hypothesis. A polished recommendation still needs evidence. Someone must establish which sources are authoritative, resolve conflicting meanings, and decide what the enterprise should do.

That is what I mean by **Human-Curated, AI-Enabled**. Curation begins with the sources and continues through interpretation, verification, and decision. It is ongoing work, not an approval click at the end of generation.

For the developing architect, the practical challenge is to learn the enterprise well enough to judge the answers. Learn why its standards exist, where its information comes from, and who carries the consequences of a decision. Then use AI to spend less time assembling the picture and more time thinking about what it means.
