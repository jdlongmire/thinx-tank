## Executive summary

- The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act (BAAJA), carried in the vehicle of H.R. 2347, by 62-33 on Thursday evening. The permitting overhaul now goes to the House.
- The Senate rejected H.Con.Res. 93, the House passed Iran war powers resolution, by 48-51. The measure would have directed the President to remove U.S. forces from hostilities against Iran absent a declaration of war or a specific authorization.
- CBO estimates the Navy's fiscal year 2027 shipbuilding plan would cost an average of $53.8 billion a year (2026 dollars) over fiscal years 2027 to 2056, including $48.9 billion a year for new ship construction. This is a CBO estimate, not an appropriation.
- The Navy awarded 28 companies a combined $7,125,820,000 multiple award IDIQ for the Surface Electronic Warfare Improvement Program (SEWIP) Family of Systems, with work possible through November 2036 if all options are exercised.
- National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the post, with Defense Department Chief Information Officer Kirsten Davies to serve in an acting capacity, according to multiple reports Thursday. Reports differ on whether the departure was voluntary.
- Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued two designation rounds: Southeast Asia cyber scam operators and their armed group facilitators, and the Comanchero gang and the 'Ndrangheta syndicate for supporting terrorist cartels.

## Senate passes the permitting bill, 62-33

The Senate passed BAAJA on Thursday evening, 62-33 (Roll Call Vote 270), completing a sequence that ran from the 73-25 vote to open debate on November 9 through final cloture at 65-31 on November 18. The bill shortens federal environmental review deadlines, narrows the window to sue over approvals, and sets cost rules for grid upgrades serving large new loads such as data centers. It now goes to the House, which takes it up after the Thanksgiving recess.

AI industry implication: permitting timelines are now the binding constraint on power and data center build speed, and the Senate has voted to loosen them. Defense implication: the same review and grid rules govern the generation and transmission projects that defense installations and defense AI data centers depend on. The votes are recorded in [the Senate Wrap Up for Thursday, November 19, 2026](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/2026/11/19/wrap-up-for-thursday-november-19-2026), and the sponsor statement is at [the Senate Environment and Public Works Committee](https://www.epw.senate.gov/press/rep/senate-passes-capitos-bipartisan-american-affordability-and-jobs-act/).

## Senate rejects the Iran war powers resolution, 48-51

Earlier Thursday the Senate voted on H.Con.Res. 93, which the House passed 220-204 on September 15. The resolution invoked section 5(c) of the War Powers Resolution and would have directed the President to remove U.S. armed forces from hostilities against Iran unless Congress enacted a declaration of war or a specific authorization for military force. It was not agreed to, 48-51 (Roll Call Vote 269). Three Republicans voted yes and one Democrat voted no, per the Senate Press Gallery account of the roll call.

Defense implication: the vote changes no law and grants no authorization, but it is the first Senate test on the Iran operation since the November 3 midterms, and it failed. The live defense funding question stays where it was: the continuing resolution expires December 11. The result is recorded in [the Senate Wrap Up for Thursday, November 19, 2026](https://www.democrats.senate.gov/2026/11/19/wrap-up-for-thursday-november-19-2026).

## CBO prices the Navy shipbuilding plan at $53.8 billion a year

The Congressional Budget Office published its analysis of the Navy's fiscal year 2027 shipbuilding plan on November 19. CBO estimates that implementing the plan, which covers fiscal years 2027 to 2056, would cost an average of $53.8 billion a year in 2026 dollars, including $48.9 billion a year for new ship construction. CBO notes the plan includes a proposal to purchase 15 nuclear powered battleships and would substantially increase purchases of large surface combatants. This is a forecast: CBO's independent estimate, not a budget decision.

Defense industrial base implication: the Senate defense draft released November 16 offers about $42 billion for shipbuilding in fiscal 2027. CBO's long run average sits well above that single year figure. Yards and suppliers planning capacity against the plan should price the gap, not the headline. The analysis is at [the Congressional Budget Office](https://www.cbo.gov/?index=7425).

## Navy awards a $7.13 billion electronic warfare vehicle

The Department of War's November 19 contract release awarded 28 companies a combined $7,125,820,000 firm fixed price, fixed price incentive, cost only, cost plus fixed fee, and cost plus incentive fee multiple award IDIQ for the SEWIP Family of Systems. SEWIP block upgrades provide early detection, signal analysis, threat warning, and protection from anti ship missiles on Navy, Coast Guard, and allied surface ships. The contract was competitively solicited with 31 offers received, and if all options are exercised, work continues through November 2036. Awardees include Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, Raytheon, BAE Systems, CACI, Leidos, General Dynamics, HII, ManTech, and Amentum, alongside smaller specialists.

Defense industrial base implication: the $7.13 billion is a shared ceiling, not obligated funding. The 28 holders now compete for orders, which spreads electronic warfare upgrade work across primes and specialists for a decade. The award is reported from the Department of War release by [GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/navy-sewip-fos-idiq-contract-award-28-companies).

## National cyber director replaced by the Pentagon CIO

Multiple outlets reported Thursday that National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross is leaving the Office of the National Cyber Director, in post since August 2025, and that Kirsten Davies, the Senate confirmed Chief Information Officer of the Department of War, will take the role in an acting capacity. A White House official confirmed the departure to several outlets. Reporting differs on the circumstances: Axios first described an amicable departure, while other sources described a dismissal. No White House statement settling that question was located in this scan, so the item is reported, not primary verified.

AI and defense implication: the change lands a week after nationwide technology outages, which the Wall Street Journal coverage ties to administration concern over cyber threats posed by new AI models, and it puts the Pentagon's enterprise technology chief in the federal cyber coordination seat. The permanent role requires Senate confirmation. The initial report is at [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/white-house-national-cyber-director-cairncross-leaving-his-post-axios-reports-2026-11-19/).

## Treasury designates cyber scam operators and cartel support networks

OFAC issued two actions on November 19. Under Executive Order 13694, as amended, and in furtherance of Executive Order 14390 on cybercrime and fraud, OFAC designated Cambodian scam center operator Zhao Hui, his companies Bei Fang Bo Lin Construction Decoration and ZHZ Investment Consulting, and Democratic Karen Benevolent Army commanders Saw Shwe Wah and Saw A One, who provide security for scam compounds in Burma. Under Executive Order 14059, OFAC designated the Australia headquartered Comanchero Outlaw Motorcycle Gang and the Italy based 'Ndrangheta, their leaders including Alexandre Varela Villar, Allan Meehan, and Angelo Figliomeni, and the Vescio funeral home network in Ontario, for logistical and operational support to terrorist cartels. U.S. property of all designated parties is blocked.

Defense and AI implication: the cyber scam round is the enforcement edge of the fraud economy that runs on stolen data and virtual currency, and both rounds add names to the screening lists that defense and technology firms must match. The actions are in [Treasury press release sb0695](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0695/) and [Treasury press release sb0696](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0696).

## The implication

The Senate voted Thursday to speed up the physical buildout and to leave the Iran operation in place, while CBO priced the fleet plan at $53.8 billion a year against a Senate draft offering about $42 billion for shipbuilding in fiscal 2027. Permits are getting faster. The arithmetic is not. Programs that plan to the authorization headline instead of the funded line will find the gap in the shipyard and in the data center queue at the same time.
