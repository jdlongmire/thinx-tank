# Research Plan and Initial Evidence Ledger

## Research objective

Establish a defensible evidence base for a Thinx-Tank method for building coherent Enterprise Architecture strategy.

The research will distinguish:

- **descriptive evidence:** how enterprises and researchers report architecture working;
- **normative frameworks:** how standards and advisory bodies prescribe EA practice;
- **Thinx-Tank synthesis:** the method developed from comparison, critique, and practical architectural reasoning.

## Initial source set

### MIT CISR — Enterprise Architecture as Strategy

Jeanne Ross, Peter Weill, and David Robertson frame enterprise architecture around an operating model and a foundation for execution. MIT CISR defines EA as the organizing logic for business processes and IT capabilities reflecting required integration and standardization. This establishes a strong research stream around operating-model choices as an antecedent to architecture.

Primary research institution: MIT Center for Information Systems Research.

Research questions:
- Is the operating model a necessary intermediate abstraction between enterprise strategy and architecture strategy?
- Which integration and standardization choices remain stable enough to guide long-lived architecture?
- How should this model be extended for digital, platform, data, and AI-intensive enterprises?

### NIST / CNSSI — Baseline, target, sequencing

NIST's CSRC glossary, citing federal and CNSSI sources, describes EA in terms of mission, required information and technologies, transitional processes, baseline architecture, target architecture, and sequencing plan.

Research questions:
- What minimum baseline is sufficient for strategic architecture?
- How should target and transition architectures be represented?
- What sequencing information is necessary to make strategy executable?

### Gartner — Future-state architecture

Recent Gartner research describes future-state architecture using six building blocks: outcomes, capabilities, delivery, governance, skills, and investments.

Research questions:
- Does this provide a sufficiently complete strategic frame?
- Where should information/data, technology constraints, security, and operating-model choices appear?
- How should these blocks connect to explicit architecture decisions?

### Gartner — Capability-based investment planning

Gartner positions capability-based planning as a way to translate business and IT strategy into technology roadmaps and investment planning.

Research questions:
- Can capability models serve as the principal traceability spine from strategy to investment?
- How do we prevent capability models from becoming static taxonomies?
- What complementary views are required for value streams, information, systems, and technology?

### Gartner — Living EA roadmaps

Gartner's 2026 roadmap guidance emphasizes concise, outcome-driven, traceable roadmaps integrated with governance and investment, rather than static detailed plans.

Research questions:
- How should roadmaps represent commitments versus options?
- What events should trigger roadmap adaptation?
- How do transition architectures relate to investment gates and delivery increments?

### MIT CISR — Enterprise IT operating models in the AI era

Recent MIT CISR research argues that AI-era operating models are affected by innovation velocity, strong IT leadership, modularity, reuse, platforms, and changing business/IT boundaries.

Research questions:
- Which architecture strategy choices become more important in AI-intensive enterprises?
- How should architecture strategy balance enterprise reuse with distributed innovation?
- What governance changes when AI capabilities cross traditional organizational boundaries?

## Initial synthesis hypotheses

These are hypotheses for testing, not conclusions.

**H1. Strategy alone is underdetermined for architecture.**
Enterprise strategy usually requires intermediate interpretation through outcomes, operating-model choices, and capabilities before architecture decisions become sufficiently constrained.

**H2. Capabilities are a useful traceability spine but not a complete architecture.**
They provide relative stability across organizational and technology change, while value streams, information, systems, technology, controls, and investments provide necessary complementary views.

**H3. A target state without transition logic is aspiration, not executable strategy.**
A coherent EA strategy requires plausible transition states, sequencing, dependencies, investments, and governance.

**H4. Architectural coherence is multidimensional.**
Traceability to strategy is necessary but insufficient. Horizontal, temporal, investment, governance, evidential, and adaptive coherence must also be evaluated.

**H5. Strategic architecture requires explicit choices.**
A strategy that only describes desired characteristics without stating trade-offs, constraints, priorities, and deliberate exclusions cannot reliably govern decentralized decisions.

**H6. Architecture strategy should be a living decision system.**
Roadmaps, standards, governance, measures, and evidence should continuously test whether architectural choices remain appropriate.

**H7. GenAI increases the feasibility of continuous EA but raises provenance requirements.**
AI can accelerate research, synthesis, traceability, scenario analysis, consistency checking, and artifact maintenance, while human architects remain responsible for source authority, semantics, judgment, disposition, and consequential decisions.

## Evidence grading

Each material conclusion should eventually be marked:

- **HIGH:** verified primary source or directly examined authoritative standard/research.
- **MEDIUM:** credible secondary source with sufficient traceability.
- **LOW:** practitioner assertion, tertiary summary, or weakly evidenced claim.
- **UNCERTAIN:** evidence insufficient or conflicting.
