# Publication Outline — Building a Coherent Enterprise Architecture Strategy

Status: pre-draft outline; research continues.

## Working title

**From Strategy to Coherence: How to Build an Enterprise Architecture Strategy That Can Actually Govern Change**

Alternative:
**Enterprise Architecture Strategy Is a System of Choices**

## Reader promise

By the end of the article, the reader should be able to distinguish an EA strategy from a target-state architecture or technology roadmap and use a practical five-stage method to construct one.

## Opening problem

Enterprises often call one of the following an "architecture strategy":

- a target-state diagram;
- a technology trend list;
- a standards catalog;
- a transformation roadmap;
- an application modernization plan;
- a TOGAF deliverable set.

Each may be useful. None, by itself, answers the strategic question:

**What architectural choices must this enterprise make so that thousands of decentralized decisions remain coherent with its intent?**

## Section 1 — Define the thing before building it

Proposed definition:

> **Enterprise Architecture Strategy is the enterprise's reasoned set of architectural choices, principles, guardrails and transition commitments that translate strategic intent into coherent capability, investment, technology and delivery decisions, with explicit mechanisms for governance, evidence and adaptation.**

Distinguish:
- enterprise strategy;
- operating model;
- EA strategy;
- enterprise architecture;
- target/future-state architecture;
- roadmaps;
- standards;
- governance.

## Section 2 — Strategy does not translate itself

Use MIT CISR primary research to establish why strategic intent often needs an operating-model layer before stable process/IT capabilities can be identified.

Then extend beyond the classic model:
- outcomes;
- capabilities;
- value streams;
- information;
- stakeholders;
- constraints;
- ecosystem;
- security;
- data/AI.

Key idea:
**Intent must be interpreted before it can constrain architecture.**

## Section 3 — The five-stage method

### 1. INTENT
What outcomes matter, and why?

Artifacts:
- architecture mandate;
- outcome statements;
- value/risk priorities;
- environmental forces.

### 2. INTERPRET
What does that intent imply for how the enterprise must operate?

Artifacts:
- operating-model decisions;
- prioritized capabilities;
- value/information flows;
- current-state evidence;
- constraints and assumptions;
- decision criteria.

### 3. CHOOSE
What architectural decisions follow?

Artifacts:
- principles;
- strategic choices;
- trade-offs;
- target characteristics;
- guardrails;
- deliberate exclusions;
- options retained under uncertainty.

This is the core of EA strategy.

### 4. EXECUTE
How do those choices change the enterprise?

Artifacts:
- gaps;
- transition architectures;
- dependency model;
- investment priorities;
- roadmaps;
- standards/reference architectures;
- governance and decision rights.

### 5. LEARN
What evidence tells us to continue, correct, or change course?

Artifacts:
- outcome measures;
- conformance/exception evidence;
- assumption tests;
- architecture health indicators;
- adaptation triggers.

## Section 4 — Eight coherence tests

Explain:
1. vertical coherence;
2. horizontal/cross-domain coherence;
3. temporal coherence;
4. investment coherence;
5. governance coherence;
6. evidential coherence;
7. adaptive coherence;
8. decision coherence.

Decision coherence is proposed as an additional test: similar decisions made independently should converge where the strategy intends standardization and legitimately diverge where autonomy is intended.

## Section 5 — Stop pretending we can predict the endpoint

Contrast classic baseline/target/sequencing discipline with current evidence about volatility.

Retain:
- baseline;
- intended direction;
- transition planning.

Refine target state into:
- commitments;
- characteristics;
- guardrails;
- options;
- hypotheses.

Key distinction:
**Commit where coherence requires commitment. Preserve options where prediction would manufacture certainty.**

## Section 6 — Architecture only matters when it changes decisions

Use empirical literature cautiously.

Show that research associates EA capabilities/maturity with agility, investment-decision preparation, alignment and other outcomes in bounded populations, while systematic review cautions against assuming EA automatically creates value.

Proposed formulation:

> EA strategy creates conditions, constraints, shared understanding, decision information and governance mechanisms through which capabilities, investments and delivery can create value.

## Section 7 — GenAI changes the economics of continuous architecture

Connect to WP-EA-HCAE-0001.

AI can:
- research and synthesize evidence;
- maintain traceability;
- detect inconsistencies;
- compare decisions with principles/standards;
- model scenarios;
- generate candidate viewpoints;
- surface assumptions and dependencies;
- monitor architecture drift.

Humans remain responsible for:
- source authority;
- semantics;
- strategic intent;
- trade-offs;
- consequential decisions;
- acceptance/rejection;
- accountability.

**Human-Curated, AI-Enabled.**

## Section 8 — Practical construction checklist

Give practitioner a compact sequence:
1. Establish mandate.
2. Define outcomes.
3. Interpret operating implications.
4. Map prioritized capabilities.
5. Establish evidence-backed current reality.
6. Identify drivers, constraints and assumptions.
7. Make explicit architecture choices.
8. Define target characteristics and guardrails.
9. Identify gaps and transitions.
10. Align investment and roadmap.
11. Encode standards and decision rights.
12. Measure outcomes and assumptions.
13. Adapt.

## Conclusion

Return to coherence.

The goal is not to predict every system the enterprise will run five years from now.

The goal is to make today's distributed decisions add up to an intentional enterprise while preserving the ability to learn and change.

**Intent → Interpret → Choose → Execute → Learn.**

Architecture becomes strategy when it governs consequential choices.
