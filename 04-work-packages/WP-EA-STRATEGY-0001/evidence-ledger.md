# Evidence Ledger — Coherent Enterprise Architecture Strategy

Status: research in progress  
WP: WP-EA-STRATEGY-0001

This ledger records sources examined, the claims they can support, limitations, and the disposition of those claims in the emerging Thinx-Tank method.

## E-001 — MIT CISR: operating model precedes stable architecture choices

**Source:** Ross, Jeanne W. (2005), *Forget Strategy: Focus IT on Your Operating Model*, MIT CISR Research Briefing, Vol. V, No. 3C. Primary institutional publication; MIT DSpace record also verified.

**Source claim:** Business strategy commonly does not provide sufficiently precise direction for stable IT and business-process capabilities. An operating model specifies required levels of process integration and standardization and thereby guides critical IT and process capabilities.

**Confidence:** HIGH.

**Disposition:** RETAIN WITH GENERALIZATION. The Thinx-Tank chain should preserve an intermediate translation layer between enterprise intent and architecture. "Operating model" is a strong candidate, but research must determine whether it is sufficient across noncommercial, mission, platform, ecosystem, and AI-intensive enterprises.

**Implication:** Supports:
Enterprise Intent → Outcomes → Operating Model → Capabilities → Architecture.

## E-002 — MIT CISR: EA is strategic organizing logic

**Source:** MIT CISR, *Classic Topics — Enterprise Architecture*; Ross, Weill & Robertson (2006), *Enterprise Architecture as Strategy*.

**Source claim:** MIT CISR defines EA as the organizing logic for business processes and IT capabilities reflecting integration and standardization requirements of the operating model and treats architecture as a strategic rather than merely technical exercise.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the CISR framework.

**Disposition:** RETAIN. This directly supports defining EA strategy around enterprise operating coherence rather than technology selection.

## E-003 — MIT CISR: architecture maturity alone is insufficient

**Source:** Ross, Jeanne W. and Quaadgras, Anne (2012), *Enterprise Architecture Is Not Just for Architects*, MIT CISR.

**Evidence:** MIT CISR reports that its 2011 survey of 146 senior IT leaders challenged earlier findings that architecture maturity itself led to business value. The later result indicated that value accrued through management practices that propagate architectural thinking across the enterprise.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the reported CISR survey finding; causal generalization should remain bounded.

**Disposition:** RETAIN AS A CRITICAL QUALIFIER.

**Implication:** The method cannot end with target architecture artifacts. Decision rights, governance, management practices, communication, standards, and organizational assimilation belong inside the strategy.

## E-004 — NIST/CNSSI: baseline, target, sequencing

**Source:** NIST CSRC Enterprise Architecture glossary, sourcing CNSSI 4009-2022, NIST SP 800-series publications, OMB Circular A-130 and prior statutory definitions.

**Source claim:** EA includes mission, required information and technologies, transitional processes, baseline architecture, target architecture, and a sequencing plan.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the authoritative definition.

**Disposition:** RETAIN, WITH MODERNIZATION.

**Implication:** Current state, target state, and transition/sequencing are necessary constructs. The research must test how detailed the baseline must be and whether a fixed target should be replaced by target characteristics plus guardrails under high uncertainty.

## E-005 — The Open Group: TOGAF as configurable scaffolding

**Source:** The Open Group, *TOGAF Standard, 10th Edition* official materials.

**Source claim:** TOGAF provides fundamental content plus modular Series Guides and is intended to be configured for different enterprise architecture practices and use cases, including agile enterprises and digital transformation.

**Confidence:** HIGH for what TOGAF claims about its own standard.

**Disposition:** RETAIN AS COMPARATIVE METHOD, NOT AS THE THINX-TANK METHOD BY DEFAULT.

**Implication:** Research TOGAF mechanisms for ADM, capability planning, transition architecture, roadmaps, governance, viewpoints, and repository discipline. Avoid importing framework mechanics merely because they are standardized.

## E-006 — Empirical evidence: EA capabilities, agility, and performance

**Source:** Hazen, B.T., Bradley, R.V., Bell, J.E., In, J. & Byrd, T.A. (2017), "Enterprise architecture: A competence-based approach to achieving agility and firm performance," *International Journal of Production Economics*, 193, 566–577. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijpe.2017.08.022.

**Evidence:** Study of 190 manufacturers modeled EA strategic orientation and EA assimilation as capabilities. Findings suggest EA-based capabilities can enhance agility and indirectly increase firm performance.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the study's reported sample and findings; MEDIUM for generalization beyond the sampled manufacturing population.

**Disposition:** RETAIN WITH POPULATION LIMITATION.

**Implication:** Supports treating EA as an organizational capability whose assimilation matters, while cautioning against universal causal claims.

## E-007 — Gartner: capability-based investment planning

**Source:** Gartner (2025), *Ignition Guide to Business Capability-Based Investment Planning*; Gartner (2025), *How Business Architects Can Help Design Strategic Portfolios Through Capability-Based Planning*.

**Source claim:** Gartner positions capability-based planning as a mechanism for translating business and IT strategy into technology roadmaps, investment planning, future-state design, and strategic portfolios.

**Confidence:** HIGH for Gartner's stated framework; empirical support not established by the accessible abstracts.

**Disposition:** TEST.

**Implication:** Supports capabilities as a potential traceability spine from outcomes to architecture and investment, but does not establish that capability models alone are sufficient.

## E-008 — Gartner: future state as outcomes plus enabling building blocks

**Source:** Gartner (2025–2026) future-state architecture research.

**Source claim:** Accessible Gartner abstracts frame future-state architecture around outcomes, capabilities, delivery, governance, skills, and investments and emphasize alignment to desired business value.

**Confidence:** HIGH for Gartner's framework description; supporting evidence is inaccessible in full research.

**Disposition:** COMPARE, DO NOT ADOPT WHOLESALE.

**Implication:** Skills, governance, investment, and delivery may need explicit representation in EA strategy rather than treatment as downstream concerns.

## E-009 — Gartner: future state as guardrails under uncertainty

**Source:** Gartner (2026), *The Future of Future-State Architectures* and *Build Future-State Architectures to Provide Guidance and Guardrails*.

**Source claim:** Gartner argues that volatile conditions weaken the usefulness of a rigid future-state destination and recommends guidance, guardrails, tiered capability development, iteration, and learning.

**Confidence:** HIGH for Gartner's stated position; MEDIUM for the proposition as a general prescription because full supporting analysis is not publicly accessible.

**Disposition:** RETAIN AS A HYPOTHESIS CONSISTENT WITH ADAPTIVE COHERENCE.

**Implication:** Target architecture may be better modeled as a combination of durable principles, target characteristics, constraints, options, and selected commitments rather than a fully predicted endpoint.

## E-010 — Gartner: roadmaps as living, outcome-driven decision assets

**Source:** Gartner (2026), *Why Most EA Roadmaps Fail and How to Build One That Works*; *Why EA Reinvention Fails Without a Pragmatic Roadmap*.

**Source claim:** Gartner advocates concise, traceable, outcome-driven roadmaps integrated with governance and investment, continuously updated as priorities change. It reports that only 26% of EA functions create roadmaps depicting interdependencies across initiative, program, and technology domains and that 41% do not provide consumable guidance.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Primary Gartner publication, but the underlying survey/sample detail for the percentages was not available in the accessible article.

**Disposition:** RETAIN THE METHOD PRINCIPLE; DO NOT LEAN HEAVILY ON THE PERCENTAGES WITHOUT UNDERLYING STUDY DETAILS.

**Implication:** Roadmaps belong inside a continuous decision system, not as static publication artifacts.

## Emerging research synthesis

The evidence currently supports a provisional architecture-strategy structure:

**Intent → Outcomes → Operating Model → Capabilities → Evidence → Drivers/Constraints → Principles → Choices → Target Characteristics/Guardrails → Gaps → Transitions → Roadmaps/Investment → Standards/Governance → Delivery → Measures → Adaptation**

Three refinements are now stronger than in the initial charter:

1. **Architecture strategy is a set of choices and management mechanisms, not simply a target-state description.**
2. **The future state should distinguish durable architectural commitments from hypotheses and options that must remain adaptable.**
3. **EA value depends on organizational assimilation and decision practices, so governance and architectural thinking cannot be treated as post-design implementation details.**

## Open evidence gaps

- Primary TOGAF text for specific capability-planning, transition-architecture, roadmap, and governance mechanisms.
- Broader empirical studies beyond manufacturing and single-organization case studies.
- Evidence comparing capability-based planning with alternative strategy-to-architecture translation methods.
- Empirical evidence for "living" versus fixed target architectures and roadmaps.
- Research on EA effectiveness in AI-intensive operating models.
- Evidence on architecture decision rights and governance as mediators of strategic outcomes.


## E-011 — Systematic review: EA value claims are frequently under-evidenced

**Source:** Gong, Y. & Janssen, M. (2019), "The value of and myths about enterprise architecture," *International Journal of Information Management*, 46, 1–9. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2018.11.006.

**Evidence:** The systematic literature review found substantial variation in what authors call EA and how value is claimed. Of the reviewed articles containing EA value claims, only a subset supplied empirical evidence. The authors argue that EA itself should not be assumed to create value directly; value depends on context-dependent mechanisms.

**Confidence:** HIGH. Peer-reviewed systematic literature review; full text examined through the TU Delft repository/Elsevier record.

**Disposition:** FOUNDATIONAL CAUTION.

**Implication:** The Thinx-Tank article must avoid claims that EA automatically improves performance. The method should identify the mechanisms by which architecture influences decisions, capabilities, investments, governance, and delivery, then measure those mechanisms and outcomes.

## E-012 — Empirical study: EA management affects agility through IT capabilities

**Source:** Pattij, M., van de Wetering, R. & Kusters, R.J. (2019), "From Enterprise Architecture Management to Organizational Agility: The Mediating Role of IT Capabilities," BLED 2019 Proceedings.

**Evidence:** Survey of 110 EA stakeholders using PLS-SEM reported that the effect of EA management on organizational agility was mediated by IT capabilities.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Peer-reviewed conference study with reported method and sample; narrower evidence base than a replicated journal literature.

**Disposition:** RETAIN AS MECHANISM EVIDENCE.

**Implication:** Reinforces the proposition that EA produces outcomes indirectly by shaping enterprise capabilities and decision conditions.

## E-013 — Empirical study: EA can improve IT investment decision preparation

**Source:** *How enterprise architecture improves the quality of IT investment decisions*, *Journal of Systems and Software* 152 (2019), 134–150. DOI: 10.1016/j.jss.2019.02.053.

**Evidence:** Quantitative study using survey data from 142 participants found that higher-performing organizations were more mature in EA and used more EA artifacts and insights in IT investment decision-making. The authors locate EA value particularly in preparation of investment decisions.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Peer-reviewed quantitative study; observational relationships should not be overstated as universal causation.

**Disposition:** RETAIN.

**Implication:** Strengthens **investment coherence** as an explicit test. EA strategy should materially shape portfolio and investment choices, not merely describe a technical future state.

## E-014 — Empirical study: EA maturity associated with multiple IT-value dimensions in U.S. hospitals

**Source:** Bradley, R., Pratt, R., Byrd, T.A. & Simmons, L. (2011), "The Role of Enterprise Architecture in the Quest for IT Value," *MIS Quarterly Executive*, 10(2).

**Evidence:** Survey responses from 140 CIOs in U.S. hospitals reported positive relationships between EA maturity and external relationship management, lower operational costs, strategic agility, business-IT alignment, and risk management.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM. Empirical study, but single industry and self-reported CIO responses constrain generalization.

**Disposition:** RETAIN WITH EXPLICIT POPULATION LIMITATION.

**Implication:** Adds evidence that architecture maturity can correlate with valuable organizational conditions while reinforcing the need to identify mediating mechanisms and context.

## Research correction

The evidence now warrants a stricter formulation of the WP's value proposition:

> EA strategy does not create enterprise value merely by existing. It creates conditions, constraints, shared understanding, decision information, and governance mechanisms through which capabilities, investments, and delivery can create value.

This distinction will govern the publication draft.
